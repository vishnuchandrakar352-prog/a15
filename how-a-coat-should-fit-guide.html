How a Coat Should Fit

A coat is worn over everything else, so it has to fit differently from a jacket. These are the checks tailors make, in the order they matter.

Shoulders first

The shoulder seam should sit at, or just beyond, the edge of your shoulder. Shoulders are the hardest part of a coat to alter, so if they are wrong, the coat is wrong. A slightly extended shoulder is fine on relaxed or oversized styles.

Room for layers

Always try a coat on over the thickest layer you plan to wear under it. Buttoned, the front should lie flat without pulling into an X shape. Reach forward: the back should move with you without straining across the shoulder blades.

Sleeve length

Sleeves should cover the cuffs of whatever you wear underneath.

  • End around the wrist bone or slightly below.
  • Your shirt or jumper should not show.
  • Sleeves are usually easy for a tailor to shorten.

Length and proportion

Hip and thigh lengths are casual and easy to move in. Knee length is the most versatile. Mid-calf and longer coats look dramatic and keep more of you warm. Shorter people often prefer a length that ends just above or below the knee.

What a tailor can fix

Sleeve length, hem length and a little shaping at the waist are straightforward. Taking in the body is possible within limits. Shoulder width, chest that pulls and a collar that stands away are hard or impossible to fix, so buy for those.
